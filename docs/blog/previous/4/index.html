East Germany

23/12/2020 0 Comments

Good evening from the former East Germany.

It's hard to say how much I enjoy the country, to be honest, because most of the time I’m alone in nature, and it feels very similar to home. When I pass through a village or town, perhaps due to the virus, the weather, and the end-of-year period, it often feels like a ghost town everywhere. I appreciate the architecture, but I can’t really get a sense of the area’s atmosphere—it’s the people who create that, and here, they are absent.

I do notice a strong difference between West and East Germany in terms of the wealth of housing, cars, and infrastructure. The west is impeccable, while the east is much less so.

So, let’s just say I like what I see most of the time, as well as my walking experience...

I have a few more weeks in East Germany, and then it will be Poland.

Yesterday, as a sort of duty to humanity, I made a detour to the former Nazi concentration camp of Buchenwald, which was later transformed by the Soviets into a special internment camp (causing more deaths than under the Nazi regime). Suffice it to say, the place has a "tense" atmosphere, and it feels incredibly heavy. It was closed due to Corona, so guided tours and access to the exhibition buildings weren’t possible. However, the entire camp itself was open, and a few explanatory panels outside provided the essentials. That was enough—I can imagine the rest.

Today, I came across ostriches. It was strange to see them at a bend in the path, in a meadow...
     I also came across a dead penguin!
     The last photo shows its corpse lying on the ground! 🤭😂

The large buildings visible just above, which look like towering communist administration or propaganda headquarters, are, in fact, a biscuit factory!

I have a fascination with twisted trees. An abnormal tree can be very beautiful... like these faces!

I also met a gentleman after Leipzig who has a collection (apparently in the world’s top 20!) of metal bottle caps, classified by country, type, and era! Like all enthusiasts, it was great to listen to him talk about his passion—especially how it all started. His three-year-old son had found a very colorful bottle cap while walking with his parents (which, from what I’ve observed, Germans do a lot. A LOT! Really... It’s such a healthy habit). Anyway, the child refused to let go of the bottle cap for several days, so the dad started bringing him a new one every day, placing it on his bedside table. And this is how, 28 years later, he ended up with a collection of over 100,000 metal bottle caps...

He also has several tens of thousands of duplicates and triplicates, all intended for exchanges...

The Alpha Point

15/12/2020 0 Comments

The Alpha Point!

I’ve just entered the former East Germany. Suddenly, there are relics and memorials everywhere. Here, a piece of the wall; there, the different barbed wire barriers and more. Antipersonnel mines were placed between each barrier all along the border—1,800,000 mines in total. Warlike dogs, intentionally starved to make them aggressive and dangerous, patrolled between the barbed wire fences. Finally, between the last fences, the ground was plowed and harrowed regularly to detect traces of any attempted crossings. All of this just to prevent its own population from leaving! 😨

Seeing this leaves a really unpleasant impression because, ultimately, it’s not that far removed from us—in any sense. There are some truly scary and sad memories here...

Fulda

6/12/2020 0 Comments

A beautiful day. I first discovered the beautiful city of Fulda, which is historically very attractive.

Then, a very nice family with whom I’ve been chatting all afternoon until now—it all started with an invitation to lunch when I asked for water to refill my bottles. I had noticed a scallop shell hanging from a nail on the facade of their house, and I thought to myself that they must be people who had made the famous pilgrimage to Saint Jacques de Compostela... or at least sympathizers of the pilgrim that I am.

And then I was offered a hot shower, a wash for my clothes, a charge for my phone’s battery, and a very friendly fight with the dog Bruno (because he’s brown).

I’m sleeping in the camper van parked in the yard tonight. It’s very practical, the bed is comfortable, and I’m indoors. This saves me from setting up and taking down my tent, which I would have otherwise placed in the garden.

We just had a traditional hot spiced wine—red wine, lots of spices, a little sugar, and that’s it. It’s a drink for this season, and yes, it keeps you warm.

Everything is okay... I’ll sleep well, even if I didn’t make much progress today. I walked maybe 10 or 12 km, but it was a beautiful day... Gray skies, but beautiful nonetheless...

Germany

27/11/2020 0 Comments

Hello!

It’s been such a long time since I last gave any news. Please forgive me. It’s not that the life of a pilgrim is so busy—though, actually, it kind of is—but more that during these coronavirus days, it’s a bit harder to recharge my phone. I need it frequently for accessing maps, so I’ve had to prioritize that over writing messages, which use up a lot of battery.

But anyway, I have to check in sometimes, and today the circumstances are just right.

So, what to say? Well, I’ve crossed France, a tiny bit of Belgium, all of Luxembourg from west to east, and now I’m in Germany—a little past Frankfurt, heading south. I think I’ll be in Germany for about three more weeks, but it’s hard to say for sure.

The weather has been changing over the past two weeks. It went from heavy rain (great for making mushrooms sprout everywhere—like, really everywhere) to cold, which sadly ended the mushroom madness. No more evening mushroom soup, and that is really sad. If you hadn’t guessed already, I’m a big mushroom fan... 😉 But to be precise, I’m a fan of whatever nature provides. It’s the generosity of nature that moves me the most, whether it’s mushrooms, nuts, apples, or berries.

The cold also stopped the rain! Youpi! It’s so nice not to be wet anymore. And soon, it will start snowing. But let’s keep that for the future...

I’m good. Happy.

I’ve reached a stage of consciousness where I can easily slip into a contemplative, automated walking state. That’s what I’m interested in, what I’m seeking—to make that state last as long as possible, maybe even permanent, rather than just fleeting moments throughout the day. But I’m happy. Everything is fine.

One thing I really enjoy is meeting so many people. It’s not that there’s a huge number, but I do meet a lot of people, and almost without exception, I encounter kindness, goodwill, and generosity. It’s such a warm and beautiful experience.

I mean, the general narrative these days is that we should be cautious when going out, that we should be suspicious or even afraid of people because “you never know.” We’re told to be careful, to “watch our six,” and so on. But my daily experience is the complete opposite.

Every day, the people I meet are incredibly kind and eager to help. They’re curious about what I’m doing and love to talk. They invite me to share meals or give me food because there might not be a shop on my route. They offer me the chance to take a hot shower in their homes (and yes, I definitely want that!).

They’re human, and it’s so nice and comforting to experience that.

Voilà.
     That’s it for today.

I hope you’re well and happy.
     Wishing you all the best.

Next, I’m sharing lots of pictures I’ve taken along the way. Hope you enjoy them! I won’t comment on them since there are so many.

Mushrooms and Orval Abbey

26/10/2020 0 Comments

Porcini mushrooms (Boletus edulis) everywhere! My God, I can't pick them all. It’s no use—my stove is broken, so I only take those I can eat raw. And of course, I limit myself to one kilo, because carrying more gets heavy, and I know I'll find more tomorrow...

Anyway, it feels strange to leave behind nice, big Corks (in France, we call them "Corks" because they resemble the shape of champagne corks). I already have 4 or 5 in my portable pantry...

Some very young porcini mushrooms (the ones in the photos were a bit too mature to eat raw without seasoning, but they were majestic. One of them must have weighed nearly a kilo—very firm), some Pinkys (our nickname in France for Agaricus campestris because of the lovely pink color under the cap), and some Airy Coprins (Coprinus comatus). It’s been a long time since I last ate those. They’re really, really good... in fricassee or soup (considering how much water they release when cooking, a bit of butter and garlic turns them into a lovely evening soup).

But since my stove is damaged, I eat them raw too. They’re tender and melt in your mouth, but of course, they don’t last long—they quickly turn into a thick black paste, almost like ink...

Also, I’ve never eaten so many nuts! All the walnut trees I come across are laden with fruit, and the ground beneath them is scattered with fallen nuts. People in the region leave them on the ground, so I take full advantage.

And the apples! I collect some every day. Apparently, no one picks them, even though they’re absolutely delicious. Far better than the dull, floury ones you find in supermarkets...

Luckily, I come across all this bounty along the way—it really eases the food stress when passing through these deserted villages. Sometimes there’s not a single grocery store for four days. As a result, my pantry empties out, only to refill with nuts, apples, and occasionally pears, as well as mushrooms.

Often, when I’m about to admit—almost ashamed—that “there’s nothing left in our villages these days,” a local will kindly offer me something to eat. How touching. Sometimes it’s a sandwich, a slice of cake, or some fruit...

I’m happy to see and experience exactly the opposite of what the media say. The usual, “My God, you have to be careful these days! Anything can happen! You can be attacked at any crossroads, robbed, raped, or even beheaded!” Instead, all I encounter is kindness, goodness, and benevolence wherever I go and whoever I meet.

Imagine this: on several occasions, total strangers have invited me to their homes to take a shower! I’m invited to come in, eat, drink coffee, wash… It really doesn’t make me want to like television or newspapers, spreading these ideas...

But it’s funny sometimes to see how even those same people—kind and welcoming—have reflexes conditioned by that mainstream, vague, and formless fear hovering over everyone:

“But aren’t you afraid?”
     “Afraid of what?”
     “Well… everything, bad encounters, all that...”
     “Well, no. Look, I’m meeting you, and it’s going well.”
     “Yes, but it’s not the same. We can see you’re a nice guy...”
     Honestly, I don’t think there are that many bad people out there. But hey, let’s stay careful—you never know... 😜

Oh, the other day, a gentleman who was vaguely part of my conversation with a mechanic couple left for ten minutes to go home. When he came back, he handed me a bag with two bananas, four clementines, two yogurts, and a packet of biscuits. I didn’t ask him for anything—I never ask—but he told me, “There’s no grocery store in the next villages you’ll pass through.”

It’s moments like these that reconcile you with society, with humanity… well, if you were ever dis-conciled. 😁

That’s all I wanted to say.

I’m doing fine. Slowly but surely, I walk the trails as much as possible. Sometimes it doesn’t rain, and even when it does, it rarely rains all day—it’s only happened three times so far.

Today, I’m staying in Orval Abbey. It’s in Belgium, right on the border. It’s nice—shower and hot meal on the agenda. Feels so good. Their beer is great.

See you soon.
     P.S. Let’s break our televisions!

France

16/10/2020 0 Comments

Here I am, staying in a communal pilgrims' house in Château-Porcien.
     Happiness!

First, a hot shower... Because biodegradable pre-impregnated wipes and a large bottle of water for washing are fine for 3 or 4 days, but after that, they're not enough... In short, a shower is definitely better and feels really good...

And then, washing clothes! Well, yes, that's necessary too... Thanks to 'genius without rubbing' (a French brand of hand washing liquid soap), where you still have to rub the clothes just the same. But hey, it is a good catchy name, isn't it? So yes, washing clothes is essential. Walking socks in humid or even wet weather, with no other drying than the natural 37-degree Celsius radiator, can create quite an odor. I don’t need to draw you a picture; olfactorily, it stings a bit and can make the eyes water...
     And then washing and drying the tent! The first time it will be dry since I left...

Finally, a patch on the inflatable ground mattress, which was punctured by a bramble stem ignominiously hidden in a comfortable tuft of grass when I dropped my backpack in the wilderness... So the last few nights were less comfortable than the first ones. The ground is damp, cold, and angular without the mattress, so 3 cm of compressed air is good, but it can burst...

In short, it's a very nice little pilgrims' house. I even cooked a hot meal tonight! And I'll have hot black coffee tomorrow morning!

And I got to recharge my phone and the additional battery.
    Happiness is easy, I tell you!

The Bizarre Scene

26/9/2020 0 Comments

Hello!

I just stopped at a camping site... So, I got a hot shower! Yay!

The 'Bois Joli' camping (the pretty grove), which is 400 m from the GR 11 (GR in France stands for 'Grande randonnée', meaning long-distance hiking trail) is located in the French Vexin (and not the Norman Vexin, which people have been insisting on, something I didn't know...), not very far from Méru... 😁

Four days of walking, just 20 km today. This is the warm-up period, and my body is adapting. So, sore feet, sore calves, sore thighs, sore shoulders... In a few days, this will pass. And the hot shower really helps. It feels really good, even if the token used doesn't really give me the time to enjoy it for long...

I am ready to head due east from now on, almost to the German border (still far away). I am therefore avoiding Paris to the north.

Did I already say that a hot shower feels good?

Today, I came across a bizarre sight where a car had gone halfway into the wall of someone's house. The homeowner had built a temporary wall around it, and the municipality reconstructed the signpost and the sidewalk, leaving the car still in place! This gives a surreal picture where the car "protrudes" from a wall onto the sidewalk without there being any sign that it was in fact an accident. Surreal, I tell you.

Then I found some nuts, mainly walnuts. I found lots and lots of nuts along the way, which often made up my meals, but here, I must admit, I had never seen such big ones... Really impressive. And good in addition to being very full. A real treat.
     Then I came across a beautiful, really beautiful church...
     See the photos.

Well, that's all for today...

I wish you well and happiness.

See you soon.

Beginning of the Pilgrimage

24/9/2020 0 Comments

Hello.

I started my pilgrimage trip 2 days ago. I'm on my way to travel about 1500 km through France, Belgium, Luxembourg, Germany, and finally Poland. This is the first step, to get warmed. The second might be much longer, I hope...

I am going slowly and stopping at every church to pray and meditate. Today I visited 4, one in each village I passed through. Beautiful places to stop, rest, and recenter a bit...

I writing to you from my small tent in a forest where I have stopped for the night. I finished my dinner, a vegetable soup cooked on my tiny stove. That really nice and comforting to eat something hot some time.

Rain is pouring. That is good because it means the mushrooms will come! 😁
What a gift it is to eat from nature, I mean to walk and find food around. Already many hazelnuts, walnuts, soon chestnuts...

That's nice.

I wish you well and happiness, and keep you in touch.
