Five years walking across Europe — 15 countries,                                                     10,000 km, 5 winters. And now, a pause...

28/8/2025

0 Comments

 
       Dear you all,

    For the past 4 years and 11 months, I have had the privilege of walking across Europe on a pilgrimage, visiting about 150 Marian places in 15 countries.

    I did it in one continuous journey, crossing France, Belgium, Luxembourg, Germany, Poland, Lithuania, Latvia, then Lithuania again, Poland again, the Czech Republic, Slovakia, Hungary, Romania, Serbia, Bosnia and Herzegovina, Montenegro, and Croatia — covering about 10,000 km. Ten thousand kilometers with my backpack, my hat, my stick, all on foot, in the tradition of pilgrimage.

     I can’t explain how beautiful and powerful that experience was. How magnificent it was to learn freedom, happiness, to cross mountains, forests, rivers, and beautiful lands. To sleep beside lakes, under the shadow of trees, on a soft mattress of moss, on sandy beaches. To meet so many kind people, receiving the warm gift of their hospitality, the incredible gift of their trust, their beautiful curiosity about me, about my way, about my approach to spirituality, to life — or simply to pilgrimage itself.

     I don’t feel as if I spent 5 years on the road. It passed in a flash, the seasons rolling like the weeks of a single month. Yes, 5 months — no more. That’s how it felt. But not for my body, not for my flesh.

     So yes, I must tell you: I decided to come back to France. I just arrived yesterday, and I am going to stay for a while before resuming my pilgrimage. I need to recover my health, which slowed me down so much over the past 2 years. And I also need to earn and save enough money for the next part.

    Because I am not done yet. Far from it. Pilgrimage is a practice. A spiritual practice. An inner quest. You know how long it can be if you truly aim to go deep, to learn truth, and to meet your Self.

    My plan — or more honestly, my wish — is to continue as soon as I am ready. I am less than halfway. There are still many destinations to reach, and my intended path was this:
Croatia, Slovenia, Austria, southern Germany, Switzerland, southern France, Italy, then by ferry to Albania, Kosovo, North Macedonia, Greece, Turkey, Lebanon, Israel, Jordan, Egypt, Libya, Tunisia, Algeria, Morocco, then by ferry to Portugal, through Spain, back to France, Belgium, the Netherlands, western Germany, and finally southern Poland — where I was initially going, where it all began. A long way still ahead.

    That is the plan, or maybe just the hope. It would have been fantastic to complete it all in one stretch. But I will wait, and prepare. Hopefully the world will have time to calm down a little, for peace to come, for the way to clear.

     If this seems sudden or rushed to you, it is not. My silence these past months may already have been a sign that I was going through hard times. And a few weeks ago, arriving in Dubrovnik, Croatia, I decided I should stop there, because I was going so slowly, with such difficulty, that it no longer made much sense to continue.

    But crossing the town, visiting its churches and resting in each one, taking something like 11 hours to cross what I could normally walk in just one, gave me new energy — and I changed my mind. I see now, five weeks later, that I actually made the decision to stop there. It took me all this time to accept it, to integrate it.

    So when the moment came to take the decision again, it was easy. Painless. Obvious. I was ready. I was sure, determined. I have no regrets. I gave everything I could. I went fully into this experience, this practice. I am at peace with myself in letting it take its course.

    I am happy. I am grateful. I feel lucky. Blessed. And contrary to what I feared, I find myself calm and serene in the middle of this restless activity — such a contrast with the 5 years of silence and solitude I just lived. Yet it does not affect me, does not disturb me. I stand in the middle of this agitation, I recognize it, and I am well. It gives me a slightly surreal feeling, a kind of detachment.

     Now I will prepare for the rest, and let you know when the time comes.

     We can stay in touch — I would love that.

     Thank you so much for everything.

     I wish you well.
     I wish you happiness.

     Emmanuel

     I am attaching many pictures from the past months, for you to see my way.
