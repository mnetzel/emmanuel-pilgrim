East Germany

23/12/2020

0 Comments

 
​     Good evening from the former East Germany.

     It's hard to say how much I enjoy the country, to be honest, because most of the time I’m alone in nature, and it feels very similar to home. When I pass through a village or town, perhaps due to the virus, the weather, and the end-of-year period, it often feels like a ghost town everywhere. I appreciate the architecture, but I can’t really get a sense of the area’s atmosphere—it’s the people who create that, and here, they are absent.

    I do notice a strong difference between West and East Germany in terms of the wealth of housing, cars, and infrastructure. The west is impeccable, while the east is much less so.

     So, let’s just say I like what I see most of the time, as well as my walking experience...

     I have a few more weeks in East Germany, and then it will be Poland.

     Yesterday, as a sort of duty to humanity, I made a detour to the former Nazi concentration camp of Buchenwald, which was later transformed by the Soviets into a special internment camp (causing more deaths than under the Nazi regime). Suffice it to say, the place has a "tense" atmosphere, and it feels incredibly heavy. It was closed due to Corona, so guided tours and access to the exhibition buildings weren’t possible. However, the entire camp itself was open, and a few explanatory panels outside provided the essentials. That was enough—I can imagine the rest.

     Today, I came across ostriches. It was strange to see them at a bend in the path, in a meadow...
     I also came across a dead penguin!
     The last photo shows its corpse lying on the ground! 🤭😂

    The large buildings visible just above, which look like towering communist administration or propaganda headquarters, are, in fact, a biscuit factory!

     I have a fascination with twisted trees. An abnormal tree can be very beautiful... like these faces!

    I also met a gentleman after Leipzig who has a collection (apparently in the world’s top 20!) of metal bottle caps, classified by country, type, and era! Like all enthusiasts, it was great to listen to him talk about his passion—especially how it all started. His three-year-old son had found a very colorful bottle cap while walking with his parents (which, from what I’ve observed, Germans do a lot. A LOT! Really... It’s such a healthy habit). Anyway, the child refused to let go of the bottle cap for several days, so the dad started bringing him a new one every day, placing it on his bedside table. And this is how, 28 years later, he ended up with a collection of over 100,000 metal bottle caps...

     He also has several tens of thousands of duplicates and triplicates, all intended for exchanges...

The Alpha Point

15/12/2020

0 Comments

 
​     The Alpha Point!

     I’ve just entered the former East Germany. Suddenly, there are relics and memorials everywhere. Here, a piece of the wall; there, the different barbed wire barriers and more. Antipersonnel mines were placed between each barrier all along the border—1,800,000 mines in total. Warlike dogs, intentionally starved to make them aggressive and dangerous, patrolled between the barbed wire fences. Finally, between the last fences, the ground was plowed and harrowed regularly to detect traces of any attempted crossings. All of this just to prevent its own population from leaving! 😨

    Seeing this leaves a really unpleasant impression because, ultimately, it’s not that far removed from us—in any sense. There are some truly scary and sad memories here...

Fulda

6/12/2020

0 Comments

 
     A beautiful day. I first discovered the beautiful city of Fulda, which is historically very attractive.

    Then, a very nice family with whom I’ve been chatting all afternoon until now—it all started with an invitation to lunch when I asked for water to refill my bottles. I had noticed a scallop shell hanging from a nail on the facade of their house, and I thought to myself that they must be people who had made the famous pilgrimage to Saint Jacques de Compostela... or at least sympathizers of the pilgrim that I am.

     And then I was offered a hot shower, a wash for my clothes, a charge for my phone’s battery, and a very friendly fight with the dog Bruno (because he’s brown).

   I’m sleeping in the camper van parked in the yard tonight. It’s very practical, the bed is comfortable, and I’m indoors. This saves me from setting up and taking down my tent, which I would have otherwise placed in the garden.

     We just had a traditional hot spiced wine—red wine, lots of spices, a little sugar, and that’s it. It’s a drink for this season, and yes, it keeps you warm.

      Everything is okay... I’ll sleep well, even if I didn’t make much progress today. I walked maybe 10 or 12 km, but it was a beautiful day... Gray skies, but beautiful nonetheless...
