Germany

27/11/2020 0 Comments

Hello!

It’s been such a long time since I last gave any news. Please forgive me. It’s not that the life of a pilgrim is so busy—though, actually, it kind of is—but more that during these coronavirus days, it’s a bit harder to recharge my phone. I need it frequently for accessing maps, so I’ve had to prioritize that over writing messages, which use up a lot of battery.

But anyway, I have to check in sometimes, and today the circumstances are just right.

So, what to say? Well, I’ve crossed France, a tiny bit of Belgium, all of Luxembourg from west to east, and now I’m in Germany—a little past Frankfurt, heading south. I think I’ll be in Germany for about three more weeks, but it’s hard to say for sure.

The weather has been changing over the past two weeks. It went from heavy rain (great for making mushrooms sprout everywhere—like, really everywhere) to cold, which sadly ended the mushroom madness. No more evening mushroom soup, and that is really sad. If you hadn’t guessed already, I’m a big mushroom fan... 😉 But to be precise, I’m a fan of whatever nature provides. It’s the generosity of nature that moves me the most, whether it’s mushrooms, nuts, apples, or berries.

The cold also stopped the rain! Youpi! It’s so nice not to be wet anymore. And soon, it will start snowing. But let’s keep that for the future...

I’m good. Happy.

I’ve reached a stage of consciousness where I can easily slip into a contemplative, automated walking state. That’s what I’m interested in, what I’m seeking—to make that state last as long as possible, maybe even permanent, rather than just fleeting moments throughout the day. But I’m happy. Everything is fine.

One thing I really enjoy is meeting so many people. It’s not that there’s a huge number, but I do meet a lot of people, and almost without exception, I encounter kindness, goodwill, and generosity. It’s such a warm and beautiful experience.

I mean, the general narrative these days is that we should be cautious when going out, that we should be suspicious or even afraid of people because “you never know.” We’re told to be careful, to “watch our six,” and so on. But my daily experience is the complete opposite.

Every day, the people I meet are incredibly kind and eager to help. They’re curious about what I’m doing and love to talk. They invite me to share meals or give me food because there might not be a shop on my route. They offer me the chance to take a hot shower in their homes (and yes, I definitely want that!).

They’re human, and it’s so nice and comforting to experience that.

Voilà.
     That’s it for today.

I hope you’re well and happy.
     Wishing you all the best.

Next, I’m sharing lots of pictures I’ve taken along the way. Hope you enjoy them! I won’t comment on them since there are so many.
