Levoca, Slovakia
     1 December 2022
     2 years, 2 months, 1 week, and 2 days on the way.
     Approximately 6575 km walked.

Today, I’ll stay inside. For today and two more nights, I am in a monastery in the center of Levoca. I need to stay in town for a bit because I have to buy some materials to repair my backpack, tent, and some parts of my clothes.

I also need to figure out how to keep my bear spray accessible while walking, without it getting in the way. Yes, bear spray is essential in Slovakia, especially if you sleep in forests and mountains as I do. No joke—there are about 2,500 brown bears in the country. That’s a lot. After doing some research and watching homemade videos from people’s homes, I decided to take the matter seriously. So, I bought a few things to reduce the risk of being attacked. The spray is one of them, but I need to find a way to have it close at hand and easily accessible.

You might say it’s unnecessary because of the season—bears are supposed to hibernate. They were, but with climate warming, bears are now seen year-round.

So, I have many things to do.
    But being inside makes it much easier. I don’t know about you, but here it’s winter. Minus 5°C all day long, dropping to minus 10°C or 12°C at night, especially in the mountains. Stitching is difficult. Actually, everything is difficult with frozen fingers.

Inside is nice. Warm. I hope not too warm, though, because I can’t sleep in a hot room anymore. Don’t get me wrong, I like the cold. I enjoy it. Minus ten doesn’t bother me—it’s beautiful, quiet, and feels pure. And I sleep very well in it. Really, it’s a pleasure. But stitching in the cold is hard!
Another thing I’ll appreciate while I’m here is hot water. Washing in rivers or mountain springs is lovely—really. But I can’t use soap then, because the water is so pure I drink it. I’m not about to put shampoo or soap in it, right? So, nature baths are nice, but incomplete. And cold—really, really cold. Painfully cold. So, they’re very brief. Brother Thomas told me I could wash my clothes here if I wanted, and of course, I do! A washing machine is a blessing!

Once I’ve done all this, I’ll walk uphill to the Mariánska Hora basilica, probably set up my tent near the water source, and enjoy a few days of meditation.
Then, I’ll be on my way again. I’ll let you know where next.

So, you see, everything is beautiful and nice. I enjoy my way as much as I hope my way enjoys me.

I wish you happiness and well-being.

Emmanuel

P.S.: Today, I received a parcel via post restante—a birthday gift from friends I met in Poland. Isn’t that lovely and kind? They sent me a high-quality power bank. It charges very fast, which is great because I’ll be able to recharge it whenever I stop at a restaurant or café. It also has a huge capacity, so once it’s fully charged, I can use it to charge my phone many times.

That’s really, really cool! And very useful to me. I’m deeply grateful for it.

So, as you can see, I’m still being taken care of on my journey, and that helps me keep going.

And so, I go...
