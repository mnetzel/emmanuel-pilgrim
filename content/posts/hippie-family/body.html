Good morning!

I’m doing really well, still immersed in this beautiful experience. I’m living a rare and precious freedom, experiencing kindness, goodness, and benevolence in almost all the exchanges I have with others, which is deeply satisfying. I revel in the noisy silence of nature and the quiet stillness of solitude. I’m living a childhood dream of escape, reliving Tom Sawyer and Huckleberry Finn.

The only possible downside is that, according to the universal law of impermanence, this will have to end at some point…
      In the meantime, I’m enjoying it to the fullest!

Today I’m staying indoors, which is nice.

I’m with a “hippie family” in northern Poland. They’re incredibly welcoming—pure peace and love. The father is no longer here. There are four children, aged 21 to 2. The mother is a tour guide in the national park! As a result, she knows the flora and fauna exceptionally well.

She makes decoctions from dandelion petals, ointments from some roots I can’t name (a local plant along with Whichstings in Ball, Whichstinks in Flower, Whichstains of the Spring, and Noideawhat in Branches), healing balms, laxative jams, regenerative juices... She walks barefoot, doesn’t drink alcohol (we’re in Poland! Some told me there were locals who don’t, but most people insisted that was a modern myth. And yet, here they are—crazy, isn’t it?).

And she doesn’t smoke either. Hippies without drugs!
     (Note: not all mushrooms are recognizable ???).

She also runs workshops on plant knowledge, glass painting, talisman and jewelry making…

Her house is an incredible mess—something between a provincial antique store, a grandfather's attic, and what you’d imagine a healer’s cabin deep in the woods might look like. There are plucked dreamcatchers and necklaces, small whitened vertebrae, horned or toothed skulls, rusty tools, and a big old nail... Antlers from deer, roe deer, and elk. Lanterns—lots of kerosene lanterns. Pots, bottles, and flasks—some not entirely empty, plump or thin, tinted and translucent, but rarely transparent due to deposits inside or outside. Carpets and cushions. Tables completely covered with no space for seats. A fish trap. Homemade candles scented with relaxing plant oils sit next to hypoallergenic, superfatted handmade soaps.

Jars of more or less dusty preserved things: a demijohn with raspberries discolored by alcohol, jars filled with who-knows-what floating in more or less liquid substances, mushrooms, shelled nuts, bouquets of dried nettles, and countless flowers—of what exactly, I couldn’t say.

The numerous shelves overflow as much as the tables and every other horizontal surface. The walls and beams are covered with all sorts of hanging items—likely suspended from nails she must have scavenged from the garden. There’s even a dangling teddy bear... or something resembling one.

When she first saw me—dragged along by an inebriated individual, Andreï, who insisted at the village grocery store that I absolutely had to come and see this place—she hugged me. I barely had time to feel self-conscious about not having used a bottle of water to wash myself beforehand. Without even asking who I was, she welcomed me in, immediately showing me where to leave my backpack and where I could sleep in one of the free dormitories. No concern for a key—it seems the place doesn’t have one.

"It's a blue house leaning against the hill. We don't knock; those who live here threw away the key long ago!" she said. (I couldn’t help but think of Maxime Le Forestier’s song San Francisco from 1974—exactly this vibe. But I guess you’d have to be an old French person to remember that big hit from the '70s.)

She pointed out a small high table, with a tray fixed to the wall next to a socket from which a kettle protruded, offering tea or coffee. She told me not to worry and that I could stay as long as I wanted, just like the all-smiling gentleman next to me, now chatting with Andreï. He had apparently arrived months ago during a bike ride and never left.

She then asked if I wanted to come with her to visit the neighbors. She was already super late (though this seemed rather normal based on her casual demeanor and what I later observed). I completely understood—my own sense of time is so blurred from walking for months that days and months feel surreal. She reassured me she’d make breakfast when we returned (yes, Andreï, it’s still morning!).

Note: At least he was only drinking beer—it could have been vodka!

There was no time to feel unsure of myself, no opportunity to feel embarrassed. Just pure, unfiltered welcome, as though I’d already been part of this place for three years. And so, I smiled.

In short, a few hundred years ago, she would have been burned in the village square by the Inquisition, or even stoned by some virtuous person in power—no doubt about it!

But now, between journalists showing up with booms and cameras at the fair where she was selling her creations and products while simultaneously teaching people how to make lucky dolls from scraps of fabric (you just sit on the pile and get started...), and the television crew set to arrive the next day for a reportage and interview, she has become well-known and appreciated—not just as a curiosity.

I saw people bringing her game animals taken during herd management, killed by hunters for the forest department. Others brought her coffee because they had just returned from Africa and thought of her. It’s truly heartwarming to see. Her daughter even told me, “She’s famous.”

At the festival/street market where we were, the stand next to hers was thrilled to be placed beside her. The vendor told me her presence would bring him more visibility and customers.

What were the chances of meeting her, of encountering this world, this reality? Seriously?

Anyway…
    Phone charging. A burning hot shower (because wood-fired heating is not thermoregulated). Clean clothes (finally—my socks were starting to sting my eyes without me even bending down!). Black coffee. A hot meal—not just bread, butter, cheese, tomato, or banana this time. (She’s a great cook, I must admit.)
     And conversations.
     Silence.
     More conversations.

Another way of living. Another way of seeing. Other ways, in general...

It’s nice.

Voilà!
     Wishing you lots of good things,
     Peace and love.
