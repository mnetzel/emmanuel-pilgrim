Hello!

Where to start? I’m well into Poland now, but my progress has been quite slow. The weather conditions are some of the coldest and harshest in years, as many people have told me. For some, it hasn’t been this cold in 20 years; for others, since their childhood. Like everywhere in Europe, winters in the past were very different from now.

I mean, I remember being stuck at home for two extra weeks of Christmas holidays because snow had blocked the roads! I remember playing sword fights with icicles in at least 40 cm of snow for a few weeks every year. But I guess that’s just a detail... Let’s say we’re having a proper, beautiful winter here.

It’s full of cold (between -10°C and -16°C during the day), full of snow (every day—sometimes very thick, sometimes soft, with heavy snowflakes at times, but more often tiny ones). The snow is so powdery that I can’t even make snowballs! I press it between my hands, and when I release the pressure, it crumbles through my fingers like dry sand.

And then there’s the wind (it feels really cold and turns those tiny snowflakes into little needles that sting any exposed skin). I guess you could call it a blizzard. I feel so lucky to walk and sleep in this beauty!

Of course, I’m wearing seven layers of clothing! Of course, I gave up on my five-finger shoes—I still need my toes! So I bought proper shoes and socks. Naturally, most of my food is now frozen: frozen oranges, frozen cheese, rock-hard butter, frozen carrots, and radishes... everything just freezes! Even the wet wipes and my shoes freeze at night.

So nice... sorry, I mean: so ice!

But that’s not why I’m so slow in my progress. It just doesn’t help. No, what’s really keeping me here for weeks when it should be days is that the Polish people I meet are dangerously welcoming! I mean, you can’t really get away! It almost always starts with a simple “Come inside for a coffee,” but then it turns into “You’ll stay for lunch!” and somehow ends up as “It’s too late to leave now; you’ll sleep here tonight and go tomorrow!”

And then it happens again the next day. Seriously! Even when we can’t communicate properly, as many people here speak fluent German or Russian but not much English, and rarely French...

I’ve set a few rules for myself during this journey, and one of them is that I shouldn’t stay in the same place for more than three nights. It’s meant to keep me moving! But I find myself using this rule almost every three days. Do the math, and you’ll understand just how much I’m not walking! That should give you an idea of how welcoming people here are.

I finally managed to arrive in a beautiful city, Wrocław (see the photos I’ve attached to this message). It’s stunning because of its architecture and history—now imagine it under 30 cm of snow! It’s been four days since I got here. I know you’re thinking, What about the three-night rule? But the rule has an exception for sickness. I’m not that rigid. Don’t worry, though—it’s nothing serious, just an ear infection. With the current weather conditions, I decided to accept the kind offer of Martin, a guy who invited me to stay in the room he rents, right next to a 93-year-old woman (an absolute master at checkers, by the way). She has no mercy for me—the crushed loser—and plays without a hint of compassion. A true checkers killer.

I met Martin while he was putting salt on the icy floor of the church where he works. I had stopped to ask if the priests offered a pilgrim shelter, and when they didn’t, Martin invited me to his place. He called a friend, bought a bottle of spiced hot wine, and voilà! Three days later, here I am—lazy and feverish (just a mild one), my right ear ringing and hurting as if I’d been slapped repeatedly.

It’s getting better now, and I’m already thinking about my departure. I’m also wondering how many days it will take me to escape the kindness and generosity here without offending anyone—except maybe the lady next door, whom I’d really like to beat at checkers before I leave!

That’s all for now.

I hope you’re well and happy.

Emmanuel.
